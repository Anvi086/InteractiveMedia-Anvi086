Interactive experience reviewed: Sculpting Harmony: The Story of the Walt Disney Concert Hall (The Getty).

1. What was the first thing you paid attention to when interacting with the experience? The first thing I paid attention to was the sound. As soon as I opened the website, a pop up came up asking me to increase the volume. After that I was drawn to the text that was moving up and down.

2. Spend two minutes with the experience and create a list of each of your discrete actions.

  • Opened the site and got a pop up asking me to turn up the volume.
  • Watched the video of the Disney Concert Hall building play on its own.
  • Read the captions that faded in over the video.
  • Scrolled down past the video.
  • Moved my cursor over the "Sculpting Harmony" text and watched it animate.
  • Kept scrolling through more moving text.
  • Scrolled into the "Chapter I: Ship in the Box" section.
  • Looked at a grid of small sketch and model images.
  • Clicked "Next" to go through the model images.
  • Pressed play/pause on a video of Gehry talking.

3. What part of the experience did you spend the most time engaging with? I spent the most time engaging with the explorable 3D study model of the concert hall interior. I kept going back to rotate and move around inside it, since being able to explore it myself made me slow down and actually study the details instead of just scrolling past.

4. What was the most common action in your two minute interaction with the experience? The most common action was scrolling. Pretty much everything I saw, the text, the video, the images, came from scrolling down, way more than clicking on anything.

5. What is your impression of the intended primary goal of the interactive experience? I think the goal is to show us the process of how Frank Gehry designed the Disney Concert Hall,rather than just showing us the finished building. That way the whole project feels very immersive and it feels as if we have been through the journey of building it together. It walks you through his sketches and models so you understand the process behind it.

6. How does the interactive experience communicate this primary goal? It uses his own voice, actual video of him talking and quotes about what he was going for, paired with the real sketches and models he made. It feels like he's personally walking you through his own process instead of someone else explaining it for him.

7. What is your impression of how the experience should be interacted with over time? (For how long and how many different times) It feels like it's made for one longer visit where you go through it start to finish, since it's set up like chapters in a story. I don't think it's really made for quick repeated visits, it wants your full attention for a while.

8. How does the interactive experience communicate how it should be interacted with over time? The chapter structure (Chapter I, II, III) and having to keep scrolling and watching to move forward makes it feel like you're supposed to go through it in order, in one sitting, kind of like reading a book or watching a documentary.

9. What other media forms (digital or otherwise) does the experience reference? It reminded me of a documentary, museum plaques or labels (the images had info next to them like title, date, and materials), architectural blueprints and sketches, and long scroll websites you'd see from a magazine.

10. What does this reference/s communicate to you about how you should act when engaging with your research experience? It made me feel like I should slow down and actually pay attention to each part instead of rushing, kind of like walking through a museum exhibit and reading the info next to each piece instead of just glancing at it.

11. What does this reference/s communicate to you about how you should feel when engaging with your research experience? It was a very immersive experience, it made me feel as if I was a part of the process.

12. What is the most frustrating part of the interaction to you and what makes that part frustrating? The most frustrating part of the interaction was having to finish watching the videos to get to the next part. It was also quite difficult to navigate.

13. What is the most satisfying part of the interaction to you and what makes that part satisfying? The most satisfying part was the text that appeared at the very beginning where it's written "sculpting harmony." When you moved the cursor over the text, it floated up. It looked super cool and totally fit the Disney vibe, it reminded me of the pixie dust from Peter Pan.
